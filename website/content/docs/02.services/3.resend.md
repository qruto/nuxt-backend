Resend sends every email the app writes and reports what happened to it. You need it for sign-in codes beyond your terminal. Its free plan covers development.

| You need | Time | Cost to start |
|---|---|---|
| A Resend account, and a domain you can add DNS records to before launch | 10 minutes, plus up to a day for DNS | Free: 3,000 emails a month |

## What it powers

| Capability | What Resend gives | Build pages |
|---|---|---|
| `auth` | Delivery of sign-in codes, invitations and new-sign-in alerts | [Sign users in](/docs/build/auth/sign-in) |
| `email` | Sending, delivery events, suppressions and idempotency keys | [Send transactional email](/docs/build/email/sending), [Track delivery](/docs/build/email/delivery) |
| `email` | Contacts, topics and segments, the base of the preference center | [Let people choose their emails](/docs/build/email/preferences) |
| `email` | Automations, the engine behind lifecycle emails | [Send lifecycle emails](/docs/build/growth/engage/lifecycle-emails) |
| `email` | Receiving, for a support inbox and the contact form | [Receive email](/docs/build/email/inbound) |

Until you add a key, sign-in still works in development: codes print in the terminal running `npx nuxt-backend dev`.

## Create the account

::steps{level="3"}

### Sign up, once per stage

Sign up at [resend.com](https://resend.com/signup). One login can belong to several teams, and each team has its own keys, domains, quota and webhooks. Create one team for production and one for development and demos: a team's webhook receives the events of every email the team sends, so a shared team mixes test events into production's.

### Create an API key

In the team's dashboard, open **API Keys** and create a key with **Full access**. The package's `email sync` creates topics, segments and the webhook, and `doctor` reads the domain's status, which a **Sending access** key cannot do. Copy the key: Resend shows it once ([Create an API key](https://resend.com/docs/create-an-api-key)).

### Add the sending domain

Open **Domains**, **Add Domain**, and enter a subdomain of yours, such as `mail.example.com`. Pick the region it sends from: `us-east-1`, `eu-west-1`, `sa-east-1` or `ap-northeast-1`. A domain keeps its region: changing it means removing the domain and adding it again ([Regions](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/regions)).

### Publish the DNS records

Add the records Resend shows at your DNS host:

| Record | Name | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| TXT | `resend._domainkey` | DKIM: the mail is signed by your domain |
| MX and TXT | `send`, under the domain | SPF and bounces, on the Return-Path subdomain |
| TXT | `_dmarc`, added by you | DMARC: what receivers do with mail that fails the two above |

Resend verifies most domains within 15 minutes, and within 72 hours at most ([Add a domain](https://resend.com/docs/add-a-domain)). Start with DMARC at `p=none`, then move it to `quarantine` once reports look clean ([Get email into inboxes](/docs/build/email/delivery/deliverability)).

::

**Who should own it.** The production team belongs to whoever owns the domain's DNS. Invite developers to the development team; few people need the production one.

**Subdomains.** Send from subdomains, never the root domain, so their reputation stays apart from your company mail. Use one for transactional mail (`EMAIL_FROM`, such as `codes@mail.example.com`) and, once you send newsletters, another for marketing (`EMAIL_MARKETING_FROM`, such as `news@news.example.com`) ([Resend on subdomains](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/is-it-better-to-send-emails-from-a-subdomain-or-the-root-domain)).

## Sandbox and test mode

Resend has no separate sandbox. The package keeps email in test mode outside production: it sends only to Resend's test addresses, which accept mail without delivering it and send real delivery events back.

| `BACKEND_ENVIRONMENT` | What the package sends | Sign-in codes |
|---|---|---|
| `development` | Test addresses only | Also printed in the terminal |
| `preview`, `sandbox` | Test addresses only | Not printed |
| `production` | Every address | Not printed |

| Address | What Resend does |
|---|---|
| `delivered@resend.dev` | Delivers it, and sends `email.delivered` |
| `bounced@resend.dev` | Rejects it, and sends `email.bounced` |
| `complained@resend.dev` | Delivers it as spam, and sends `email.complained` |
| `suppressed@resend.dev` | Suppresses it, as for an address that bounced before |

All but `suppressed@` take a `+label`, such as `delivered+signup@resend.dev`. Test sends count against your quota. Before your domain is verified, `onboarding@resend.dev` works as the sender, to your own address only ([Send test emails](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/send-test-emails)). `npx nuxt-backend inbox` reads what was sent ([Read test mail in the dev inbox](/docs/develop/dev-inbox)).

## Keys and environment

| Variable | Where to find it | Dev | Prod | Secret |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `EMAIL_API_KEY` | **API Keys**, a full-access key of that stage's team | The development team's key | The production team's key | Yes |
| `EMAIL_FROM` | An address on your verified domain, alone or as `Name <address>` | `onboarding@resend.dev` until the domain verifies | `Panels <codes@mail.example.com>` | No |
| `EMAIL_MARKETING_FROM` | An address on the marketing subdomain. Arrives with ENGAGE | Optional | Needed once you send newsletters | No |
| `EMAIL_WEBHOOK_SECRET` | The webhook's signing secret, `whsec_…`. `email sync` stores it | Stored by the sync | Stored by the sync | Yes |
| `EMAIL_REGION` | The region you picked for the domain | Optional | The domain's region | No |

All of them live on the Convex deployment. `EMAIL_REGION` records the region the domain sends from; Resend picks the region by the domain, so the value must match it.

## Link it

::steps{level="3"}

### Push the key and the sender

```bash [.env.local]
EMAIL_API_KEY=re_your_development_key
EMAIL_FROM=onboarding@resend.dev
```

```bash
npx nuxt-backend env push
```

### Sync topics, segments and the webhook

```bash
npx nuxt-backend email sync
```

It creates the topics, the segment and the contact properties `backend/email.ts` declares, and the lifecycle automations you turned on. It points a webhook at the deployment's `/email/events` with every event the package reads, and stores the signing secret as `EMAIL_WEBHOOK_SECRET` ([`email sync`](/docs/reference/cli#email-sync)). A local deployment needs a tunnel: pass its URL with `--webhook https://your-tunnel-host/email/events`.

### Send yourself a test

Sign in with `delivered+you@resend.dev`, then run `npx nuxt-backend inbox`: the code is there.

::

Then run [`npx nuxt-backend email sync`](/docs/reference/cli#email-sync): it points a webhook at `https://<slug>.convex.site/email/events` with every event the package reads, and stores its signing secret on the deployment as `EMAIL_WEBHOOK_SECRET`. To do it by hand instead, add the endpoint in **Webhooks**, copy its signing secret to `.env.local` as `EMAIL_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, and run `npx nuxt-backend env push`.

## Check it

```bash
npx nuxt-backend doctor --only email
```

| Finding | What it checks |
|---|---|
| [`email-transport`](/docs/reference/cli/diagnostics#email-transport), [`deployment-email-api-key`](/docs/reference/cli/diagnostics#deployment-email-api-key) | A key is set, and Resend accepts it |
| [`deployment-email-from`](/docs/reference/cli/diagnostics#deployment-email-from) | The deployment has a sender |
| [`email-webhook-secret`](/docs/reference/cli/diagnostics#email-webhook-secret), [`email-webhook-route`](/docs/reference/cli/diagnostics#email-webhook-route) | Delivery events reach the deployment and pass the signature check |
| [`email-sender-domain`](/docs/reference/cli/diagnostics#email-sender-domain) | Under `--prod`: the sender's domain is verified |
| [`email-reputation`](/docs/reference/cli/diagnostics#email-reputation), [`email-quota`](/docs/reference/cli/diagnostics#email-quota) | Under `--prod`: last week's bounces and complaints, and the quota left |

## Go to production

::steps{level="3"}

### Verify the production domain

Add it to the production team, publish its records, and wait for **Verified**.

### Set production's key and sender

```bash
npx convex env set --prod EMAIL_API_KEY
printf %s 'Panels <codes@mail.example.com>' | npx convex env set --prod EMAIL_FROM
```

### Sync and check

With the production `CONVEX_DEPLOY_KEY` and key in your shell:

```bash
npx nuxt-backend email sync --prod
npx nuxt-backend doctor --prod
```

::

Leave the free plan before launch: its 100 emails a day can run out on launch day, and every sign-in code is an email. `doctor --prod` warns while the account has a daily quota.

## Costs and limits

Checked on 2026-10-25 against [Resend pricing](https://resend.com/pricing) and its [limits](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/account-quotas-and-limits):

| | Free | Pro | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | $0 | $20 a month for 50,000 emails, $35 for 100,000 | From $90 a month for 100,000 emails |
| Daily cap | 100 emails | None | None |
| Domains | 3 | 10 | 1,000 |
| Webhook endpoints | 1 | 5 | 10 |

- **Marketing email** is priced by contacts: free up to 1,000, then from $40 a month for 5,000.
- **Rate limit:** 10 requests a second per team, shared by every key. Support can raise it ([Rate limit](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/rate-limit)).
- **Overage** on paid plans costs $0.90 to $0.46 per 1,000 emails, capped at five times your quota.
- **Webhooks:** Resend retries a failed delivery for about a day and a half, then disables an endpoint that keeps failing and emails the team. `doctor` reports a disabled one.
- **Idempotency keys** last 24 hours: a send retried with the same key goes out once.

## Data and compliance

- **What it holds:** every recipient's address, each email's content, its delivery events, and contacts with their topic choices.
- **Region:** mail leaves from the domain's region, but Resend stores all account data in the US, whichever region you send from. Forge, Resend's own mail server, runs in `us-east-1` only ([Forge](https://resend.com/docs/forge)).
- **Retention:** email content, events and logs are kept for 30 days on every plan.
- **Role:** Resend processes data on your behalf. Its [DPA](https://resend.com/legal/dpa) covers GDPR, and it lists its [subprocessors](https://resend.com/legal/subprocessors), all in the US. It holds SOC 2 Type II ([Security](https://resend.com/docs/security)).
- **Erasure:** deleting an account removes its Resend contact too ([Export and delete an account](/docs/build/auth/accounts/data)).

Name Resend in your privacy policy as a processor that transfers data to the US ([Privacy, GDPR and cookies](/docs/ship/compliance/privacy)). Email law has its own page: [Follow email law](/docs/ship/compliance/email-law). This is not legal advice.
