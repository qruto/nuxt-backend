Polar runs checkout, subscriptions, credits and discounts, and sells to your customers as the merchant of record. You need it for billing. Its sandbox is free.

| You need | Time | Cost to start |
|---|---|---|
| A Polar sandbox account now, a production account before launch | 10 minutes for the sandbox; up to 14 days for the production review | Free. Production takes a fee per transaction |

## What it powers

| Capability | What Polar gives | Build pages |
|---|---|---|
| `billing` | Products, checkout, subscriptions, the customer portal and invoices | [Set up billing](/docs/build/billing/setup), [Manage subscriptions](/docs/build/billing/subscriptions) |
| `billing` | Meters and credit grants, the balance behind credits | [Sell credits and top-ups](/docs/build/billing/credits) |
| `billing` | Discounts and checkout links | [Run a promotion](/docs/build/growth/offers) |
| `ai` | The credit meters each metered model call spends | [Meter an AI action](/docs/build/ai/metered-actions) |

The package keeps a copy of each customer's plans, features and balances in Convex, fed by Polar's webhook, so your pages never wait on Polar ([React to billing events](/docs/build/billing/events)).

## Create the account

Polar runs two separate servers: the sandbox for development and production for real money. Each has its own accounts, organizations and tokens. A token from one never works on the other.

::steps{level="3"}

### Sign up in the sandbox

Open [sandbox.polar.sh/start](https://sandbox.polar.sh/start), sign in with GitHub, Google or an email address, and create an organization. Name it after your app: customers see the name at checkout.

### Create an organization access token

In the organization's **Settings**, scroll to **Developers** and choose **New Token**. Give it a name, an expiry and every scope: `billing sync` creates products, benefits, meters, discounts and the webhook endpoint. Workspaces need its `members:write` and `customer_sessions:write` scopes, for team customers and each member's portal. Copy the token: Polar shows it once.

### Sign up for production when you are ready to sell

At [polar.sh/signup](https://polar.sh/signup), create the production organization the same way. Then open **Finance**, **Account**, and complete the review: your business details, the owner's identity check through Stripe Identity, and a payout account through Stripe Connect. Polar reviews within 14 days, often within 7 ([Account reviews](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/account-reviews)). Read the [acceptable use policy](https://polar.sh/legal/acceptable-use-policy) first: Polar decides what it will sell for you.

::

**Who should own it.** The person who answers for the company's money owns the production organization and its payout account. Invite the developers as members. A sandbox organization can belong to anyone on the team.

## Sandbox and test mode

The sandbox runs the same API as production, with no real money. Check out with the card `4242 4242 4242 4242`, any future expiry date and any CVC.

| `BACKEND_ENVIRONMENT` | Polar server | What differs |
|---|---|---|
| `development`, `preview`, `sandbox` | The sandbox, `sandbox-api.polar.sh` | No money moves. Polar's own emails reach only members of the organization, `+` aliases included. The API allows 100 requests a minute |
| `production` | Production, `api.polar.sh` | Real cards, real payouts, 500 requests a minute |

A sandbox organization has ids of its own, so `billing sync` writes the sandbox's and production's ids side by side in `backend/billing.generated.ts`. [Develop on real sandboxes](/docs/develop/sandboxes#billing-on-polars-sandbox) walks the first checkout.

## Keys and environment

| Variable | Where to find it | Dev | Prod | Secret |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BILLING_ACCESS_TOKEN` | **Settings**, **Developers**, **New Token**, in the organization of that server | The sandbox organization's token, in `.env.local` | The production organization's token, on the production deployment and in your shell for the sync | Yes |
| `BILLING_WEBHOOK_SECRET` | Stored on the deployment by `billing sync` when it creates the endpoint | Set by the sync | Set by the sync | Yes |

Both live on the Convex deployment. The Nuxt app needs neither.

## Link it

::steps{level="3"}

### Push the token

```bash [.env.local]
BILLING_ACCESS_TOKEN=your_sandbox_access_token
```

```bash
npx nuxt-backend env push
```

### Create the catalog and the webhook

```bash
npx nuxt-backend billing sync
```

It creates the meters, benefits, plans and credit packs of `backend/billing.catalog.ts` in the sandbox organization, and writes their ids to `backend/billing.generated.ts`. It also creates a webhook endpoint at the deployment's `/billing/events`, subscribed to every event the package handles, and stores its signing secret as `BILLING_WEBHOOK_SECRET` ([Define and sync your catalog](/docs/build/billing/catalog)).

### Reach a local deployment through a tunnel

Polar cannot reach a deployment on your machine. A cloud dev deployment needs nothing more. For a local one, run a tunnel to its HTTP origin and pass its URL:

```bash
npx nuxt-backend billing sync --webhook https://your-tunnel-host/billing/events
```

::

Polar signs deliveries with Standard Webhooks for secrets created on or after 2026-09-08, and with its own HMAC before that. The package accepts both, so an older secret keeps working ([Register webhooks](/docs/ship/security/webhooks)).

## Go to production

::steps{level="3"}

### Finish the account review

Polar holds payouts until the review passes. Start it a week or two before launch.

### Set production's token

```bash
npx convex env set --prod BILLING_ACCESS_TOKEN
```

It asks for the value. Export the same token in your shell for the next step.

### Sync the catalog to production

With the production `CONVEX_DEPLOY_KEY` in your shell:

```bash
npx nuxt-backend billing sync --env production
```

It writes the `production` ids beside the sandbox's, creates production's webhook endpoint and stores its secret. Commit `backend/billing.generated.ts` and deploy it.

### Check it

```bash
npx nuxt-backend doctor --prod
```

::

Leave Polar's customer emails on: receipts, renewal reminders, failed payments and cancellations come from Polar, not from the package ([Customer emails and notifications](/docs/build/billing/events/customer-emails)).

## Costs and limits

Checked on 2026-10-25 against [Polar's fees](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/fees):

| Plan | Monthly | Per transaction |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | $0 | 5% + 50¢ |
| Pro | $20 | 3.8% + 40¢ |
| Growth | $100 | 3.6% + 35¢ |
| Scale | $400 | 3.4% + 30¢ |

- **Extra fees:** cards issued outside the US add 1.5%. A dispute costs $15 whatever its outcome. A refund does not return the fee.
- **Payouts:** Stripe's own fees apply: $2 per active month, 0.25% + 25¢ per payout, and 0.25% to 1% for currency conversion.
- **Organizations created before 2026-05-27** keep the early-member rate of 4% + 40¢, plus 0.5% on subscriptions.
- **API rate limits:** 500 requests a minute per organization in production, 100 in the sandbox. Past it, Polar answers `429` with `Retry-After`.
- **Webhooks:** Polar retries a failed delivery up to 10 times, and disables an endpoint after 10 failures in a row. `doctor` reports a disabled one, and you can [replay what it missed](/docs/ship/operate/replay).

## Data and compliance

- **Role:** Polar is the merchant of record. It sells to your customer, collects the payment and remits VAT, GST and US sales tax ([Merchant of record](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/introduction)). You still owe your own income tax.
- **What it holds:** each customer's name, email address, billing address, payment details (through Stripe), orders, subscriptions and invoices.
- **Region:** its [privacy policy](https://polar.sh/legal/privacy) says data may be stored in the US, Canada or elsewhere outside the EEA and the UK. There is no region to choose.
- **DPA:** Polar publishes no DPA at a stable address as of 2026-10-25. Ask Polar for one before you sign customers in the EU.
- **Erasure and export:** deleting an account deletes its Polar customer with `anonymize=true`, which cancels its subscriptions and anonymizes its personal data ([Export and delete an account](/docs/build/auth/accounts/data)). Customers can export their own data from Polar's customer portal.

Name Polar in your privacy policy, and link its terms at checkout ([Privacy, GDPR and cookies](/docs/ship/compliance/privacy)). This is not legal advice.
