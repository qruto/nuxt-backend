- **You get:** images checked by a provider made for it, a second opinion where a score is close, known abuse material stopped before anything else sees it, one set of categories and thresholds across providers, and a statement of reasons for every decision.
- **You do:** create each provider's account, set its key on the deployment, and name the providers in `setupAi({ moderation })`.

[Content safety](/docs/build/ai/content-safety) checks text with a safety model through your gateway. That model reads images too where the Convex AI Gateway serves Llama Guard 4, but a model of its kind is no image moderation service: it scores no nudity in artwork, and it knows no list of known abuse material. The providers on this page take the images instead.

## Name the providers

```ts [backend/ai.ts]
import { arachnidShield, azureContentSafety, openaiModeration, setupAi } from 'nuxt-backend/ai'

export const ai = setupAi(components, {
  billing,
  moderation: {
    images: [azureContentSafety({ region: 'francecentral' })],
    secondOpinion: openaiModeration(),
    knownAbuse: arachnidShield(),
    policy: { version: '2026-11-08' },
  },
})
```

A check then runs in this order:

1. **The known-abuse match**, on every image, before any other provider sees its bytes. A match blocks the content whatever `block` says, escalates it, sends the image nowhere else, and records its SHA-256, never the image. Only an explicit "no known match" clears an image: an answer Shield gives with no classification it knows refuses the content as unchecked. `knownAbuse` needs `log`, so `setupAi` refuses it beside `log: false`.
2. **The text classifier** reads the text, and **the image providers** read the images. With no image provider named, the classifier reads the images too, as before. No image provider here judges `minors_sexual` in an image, so a classifier that reads images (Llama Guard 4 on the Convex AI Gateway) also reads them for that one category, and only its `S4` counts.
3. **The second opinion**, only when a score sits just under its threshold (within 0.15). It can raise a score, never lower one.

Any provider flagging a category blocks it. A provider that fails, times out or answers something it cannot read refuses the content as unchecked (`unavailable`, reason `provider_refused`), which a job tries again. Each provider's key goes on the deployment, named after what it does:

| Provider | Factory | Env | Reads | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Azure AI Content Safety | `azureContentSafety({ region })` | `CONTENT_SAFETY_ENDPOINT`, `CONTENT_SAFETY_KEY` | Text, images | Hate, sexual, violence and self-harm at 4 severities for images, artwork included. Your own resource, in the region you choose; inputs are neither stored nor trained on. Images up to 4 MB and 7200 px a side: a larger one is refused `invalid_input` (reason `too_large`), naming the stage, so downscale a model's output before you check it. No check for abuse material |
| OpenAI moderation | `openaiModeration()` | `SECOND_OPINION_KEY` | Text, images | `omni-moderation-latest`, free. For images it scores sexual content, violence and self-harm only. Never send it suspected abuse material |
| Project Arachnid Shield | `arachnidShield()` | `KNOWN_ABUSE_KEY` | Images | A hash match against known child sexual abuse material, from the Canadian Centre for Child Protection, free once your organization is vetted |
| Llama Guard 4 | `text` (the default) | Through your AI gateway | Text, images | The 14 MLCommons hazards. See [Content safety](/docs/build/ai/content-safety) |

Planned, not built yet: Hive, Google Cloud's content safety (Vision SafeSearch), Amazon Rekognition and Sightengine for images, and Microsoft PhotoDNA as a second known-abuse match. None is exported until its adapter ships.

Sources: [Azure AI Content Safety](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-services/content-safety/overview), [OpenAI moderation](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/moderation), [Project Arachnid](https://www.projectarachnid.ca/). The Arachnid Shield adapter follows the service's published API, and has not been run against a live account yet.

## Read the categories

Every provider maps its answer onto one list, so `block`, `escalate` and the thresholds read the same whichever provider answered:

| Category | Azure | OpenAI | Llama Guard 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sexual` | Sexual, severity 4 or more | `sexual` | S12 |
| `suggestive` | Sexual, severity 2 | Not judged | Not judged |
| `minors_sexual` | Not judged | `sexual/minors`, text only | S4 |
| `violence` | Violence | `violence`, `violence/graphic` | S1 |
| `self_harm` | SelfHarm | `self-harm/*` | S11 |
| `hate` | Hate | `hate/*`, text only | S10 |
| `weapons` | Not judged | `illicit/violent`, text only | S9 |
| `drugs` | Not judged | `illicit`, text only | S2 |
| `csam_match` | No | No | No: Arachnid Shield alone |

A category a provider cannot judge is kept as not judged, never as a score of 0: OpenAI answers 0 for `sexual/minors` in an image, and that never reads as safe.

A verdict carries `categories` (the shared ones at or above their threshold), `codes` (each provider's own, such as `S12` or `Sexual:6`), `scores` and `providers`. `block` and `escalate` take either:

```ts [backend/ai.ts]
moderation: {
  images: [azureContentSafety({ region: 'francecentral' })],
  block: ['sexual', 'minors_sexual', 'csam_match', 'S4'],
  escalate: ['minors_sexual'],
  thresholds: { violence: 0.6 },
}
```

The default thresholds suit an app that takes no sexual content, as card processors ask of AI image generation: `sexual` 0.3, `minors_sexual` 0.05, `suggestive` 0.7, and `violence`, `self_harm` and `hate` 0.5. At `stage: 'prompt'` or `stage: 'output'`, `sexual` and `suggestive` default to 0.05, any signal, since no hash list can help with what a model makes. A threshold you name wins there: an app that draws swimwear or a kiss in its comics sets `suggestive: 0.7`, or every such image Azure rates severity 2 is blocked. `minors_sexual` never goes above 0.05 at those stages.

With an image provider and no `block: 'all'`, `block` must cover `sexual` (or `S12`): child sexual abuse material no hash list knows is otherwise caught only where the classifier reads images, so `setupAi` refuses a policy that would let it through.

## Give people their statement of reasons

Every flagged check is recorded with the providers, the scores, the thresholds, your `policy.version`, and that it was decided by automated means. That is what the DSA's Article 17 asks a statement of reasons to hold. `getModerationStatement` gives it to the person the content came from, and to administrators:

```ts [backend/ai.ts]
export const { getCosts, listModerationFlags, getModerationStatement } = ai.api
```

```vue [app/pages/statement/[id].vue]
<script setup lang="ts">
import { api } from '#backend/api'

const route = useRoute()
const statement = useQuery(api.ai.getModerationStatement, () => ({ flagId: route.params.id as string }))
</script>
```

It answers `{ decision, automated, categories, providers, policyVersion, subject, stage, at }`. Your page adds the redress: how to appeal, and to whom.

Suspected child sexual abuse material gives the person who uploaded it no statement: a known-abuse match, and a flag on legal hold for `minors_sexual` or `S4` from a classifier. Telling them could warn them off an investigation, so report it to the authorities instead ([Follow the CSAM playbook](/docs/ship/compliance/moderation/csam-playbook)); an administrator still reads the statement. Whether to withhold it is a legal call for you, the operator: this is not legal advice.

## Know what the law asks

A small host in the EU owes, under the Digital Services Act:

- **Article 16:** a notice form anyone can use to report illegal content ([Moderate user content](/docs/ship/compliance/moderation)).
- **Article 17:** a statement of reasons for every restriction, as above.
- **Article 18:** a report to the authorities of any information about a crime that threatens someone's life or safety.
- **Article 14:** terms that describe the automated tools you moderate with.

Scanning uploads is not something the law asks of a host: the interim derogation (Regulation 2021/1232) covers interpersonal communications services only. Card processors and payment providers do expect it of an app that generates or hosts images. This is not legal advice.

## Check it

`npx nuxt-backend doctor` reports [`ai-moderation-providers`](/docs/reference/cli/diagnostics#ai-moderation-providers): each provider's keys on the deployment, a known-abuse match beside image checks, an EU region for Azure when `inferenceRegion: 'eu'` pins your processing there, and where new abuse material in an image is caught when no image provider judges minors.

In tests, `nuxt-backend/test` answers every provider with a fake: `TEST_UNSAFE('sexual')` in text or an image's bytes finds that category, `TEST_FAINT('violence')` finds it faintly, and `TEST_KNOWN_ABUSE` matches the list. No call leaves the test.
