The packaged UI is built to work with a keyboard, a screen reader and speech input out of the
box, and the package's tests hold it there. This page says what it guarantees, what is tested,
and what stays yours. It is not a conformance claim: automated checks find some problems, not all
of them, and your app around the components is part of every page.

## What every component guarantees

- **Focus moves only when the person moved it.** When you advance a step (send a code, open a
  rename field, switch workspaces), focus lands on what you act on next. A change the component
  makes by itself leaves focus where it is.
- **Errors are tied to their field.** A refused field is `aria-invalid`, and `aria-describedby`
  points at the sentence that says why, so a screen reader reads it on the field.
- **Controls are named by what they show, and placed in context.** A button's accessible name is
  its words, so speech input finds it by what it says, in whatever words your labels or
  translations give it. A row of the same words does not read as five "Subscribe"s: each sits in a
  group named by what it acts on (a plan card, a workspace row, a charge, a gift), and that name
  describes the button too.
- **Busy buttons keep focus and say what they wait for.** A busy button is `aria-disabled`, not
  `disabled`, so focus stays on it; it reads *"Opening checkout…"* or *"Canceling…"*, never a bare
  ellipsis, a status announces the same, and the part of the page that waits is `aria-busy`.
- **Live regions announce what changed.** Loading notes, empty notes and outcomes are polite
  statuses, which wait their turn; a failure is an alert, which interrupts, with no empty note
  beside it. A banner appears inside a live region that was there all along, so it is announced.
- **The page declares its language.** Every packaged page sets `<html lang>` to the active
  locale, English without `@nuxtjs/i18n`.
- **Every field has a label.** A visible one, or an `aria-label` where the row has no room for one.
- **Keyboard focus is visible.** The module's stylesheet draws a focus ring, in the accent token,
  on every button and link inside the packaged components. The billing links sit in your markup
  and take your app's link styles, focus ring included.

## The contract: WCAG 2.2 AA

The packaged UI targets [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) at level AA, the level the European Accessibility Act points to through its harmonised standard (checked on 2026-10-25). Beyond the guarantees above, 2.2 adds what the components keep:

- **Focus not obscured (2.4.11).** The notices region, the shell's header and the connection status never cover the focused control.
- **Target size (2.5.8).** Every control is at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, or spaced as the criterion allows.
- **Dragging movements (2.5.7).** Drag and drop in `<UploadField>` always has a click or keyboard path.
- **Accessible authentication (3.3.8).** Sign-in asks for no memorized secret and no puzzle: a passkey, or a code the browser can fill as a one-time code and the person can paste.
- **Redundant entry (3.3.7).** A step never asks again for what the person already typed in the same flow.

The headless composables carry the same attributes in their bindings, and `#backend/a11y` exports the helpers the components use (`useActionState`, `useFocusReturn`, `useLiveRegion`), so your own markup can keep the contract ([Build on the composables](/docs/build/ui/customize/headless)). The `accessibility` page key serves an accessibility statement you fill in ([Meet the European Accessibility Act](/docs/ship/compliance/accessibility)). Not legal advice.

## What is tested

- **The component contract**, on every component in every state its row lists
  ([Components](/docs/reference/components) has them):
  - every element carries the component's `data-*` family;
  - every control has an accessible name with a word in it, and a name set by `aria-label` starts
    with the words the control shows;
  - two enabled buttons share a name only in differently named groups or with different
    descriptions, and every `aria-describedby` points at an element that exists;
  - loading is `aria-busy` with a status that says so in words; empty is a status or nothing at
    all; a failure is an alert with no empty note beside it; a pending action is `aria-busy`;
  - a button pressed into its pending state keeps focus, is `aria-disabled` and says something
    new;
  - a refused caller sees none of the controls they may not use;
  - every slot renders in some state.
- **An axe-core smoke** on the starter app's production build, in a real browser, in the light and
  the dark scheme: `/login` at each step a signed-out visitor reaches (choosing a way in, asking
  for a code, entering it), `/pricing`, and the page guard's 403 with `<AccessDenied>`, signed in.
  It runs every non-experimental WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 rule at levels A and AA that axe-core has,
  contrast included, and fails on any violation, and on any contrast check axe could not finish.
  Each new module page adds its steps to it.

Neither replaces a person with a screen reader. Report what you find on
[GitHub](https://github.com/qruto/nuxt-backend/issues).

## What a replacement must keep

When you replace a region through a [slot](/docs/build/ui/customize/slots) or rebuild a page from the
composables, the guarantees above are yours to keep for that region:

- name every button by its words, and put a repeated one in a group named by what it acts on;
- keep focus on a busy button (`aria-disabled`, not `disabled`), say what it waits for, and
  mark the waiting region `aria-busy`;
- announce loading and empty as a status and a failure as an alert;
- tie a field's error to it with `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby`;
- move focus only when the person moved on.

## What this means for you

The document around the components is yours. The packaged pages declare their language; declare
it for your own pages (`app.head.htmlAttrs.lang` in `nuxt.config`, or `@nuxtjs/i18n`'s locale
head). Declare `color-scheme` on the root so the tokens follow light and dark, and give the page
an explicit background and text colour (`background: Canvas; color: CanvasText` follows the
scheme), so a contrast checker reads the real pair rather than assuming white. Keep a `<main>`
landmark where your layout has one: the packaged pages render theirs. When you restyle with
[tokens](/docs/build/ui/customize/tokens), check the contrast of your own colours: the defaults pass
WCAG 2.2 AA in both schemes, yours have to as well. The pages around the components, your copy and
your images are part of every page an audit reads: the statement says what you checked.

In 0.2.0 the four boundaries became one [gate](/docs/build/ui/gate) and the four banners one
[notices region](/docs/build/ui/notices), each keeping the guarantees of its rows above, and the agent
consent screen left with the end-user MCP endpoint. The new components, such as the
[app shell](/docs/build/ui/app-shell) and the [upload field](/docs/build/data/files/checked-uploads), join the
table with their own rows when they land, and the smoke with their pages.
