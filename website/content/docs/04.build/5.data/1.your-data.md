Your tables live in your own schema, `backend/schema.ts`, beside the tables the `backend` component keeps. Convex gives you the database, its indexes and transactions, and keeps a component's tables apart from yours. The package adds the access tiers that guard your functions, the account and workspace ids they hand you, and the sources that join your tables to a person's export and erasure.

## The model

```text
your deployment
├─ your app             backend/schema.ts and backend/*.ts
│    └─ reaches the package through nuxt-backend/* and components.backend.*
├─ backend component    accounts, workspaces, billing cache, files, activity trail
└─ official components  workflow, workpool, and any your app mounts itself
```

Convex keeps a component's tables apart from the app's. The app cannot query a component's tables, and a component cannot query the app's. So the package's data and yours never mix, and each side reaches the other only through functions. Your schema holds only your tables: the scaffold writes no `schema.ts`, and you add one with your first table.

A row refers to the package's records by their ids. To your schema those ids are strings, because a `v.id()` validator names a table of your own schema. The access tiers hand you both ids, so you never read them from a client's arguments:

| Id | Where you get it | Store it as |
|---|---|---|
| The account | `ctx.user.id`, in every tier | `userId: v.string()` |
| The workspace | `ctx.workspace.id`, in the `workspace` and `withFeature(key)` tiers | `workspaceId: v.string()` |

## Define a table

```ts [backend/schema.ts]
import { defineSchema, defineTable } from 'convex/server'
import { v } from 'convex/values'

export default defineSchema({
  strips: defineTable({
    workspaceId: v.string(),
    authorId: v.string(),
    title: v.string(),
  }).index('by_workspaceId', ['workspaceId']),
})
```

Read through an index rather than a filter. A filter still reads every row in the index range, so only an index keeps a read small as the table grows. Name an index after its fields (`by_workspaceId_and_title`). Return a bounded list: `take(n)`, or a page at a time with [pagination](/docs/build/data/read-write#pagination). The Convex guide to [indexes](https://docs.convex.dev/database/reading-data/indexes/) covers the rest.

## Guard every function

Anyone who reaches the deployment can call every function you export. So declare each one at the tier it needs, from the builders `backend/functions.ts` exports. A tier checks the caller before your handler runs, and refuses with a [typed error](/docs/build/data/read-write/errors) otherwise:

| Tier | Who may call | Hands you |
|---|---|---|
| `authed` | A signed-in account that is not banned | `ctx.user` |
| `workspace` | A member of the active workspace, checked fresh on every call | `ctx.user`, `ctx.workspace` (`id`, `slug`, `name`, `role`) |
| `admin` | An application administrator | `ctx.user` |
| `withRole(role)` | An account with an app-wide role of yours | `ctx.user` |
| `withFeature(key)` | A member of a workspace whose plan has the feature | `ctx.user`, `ctx.workspace` |

A mutation or an action can also ask for a recent sign-in. [Protect backend functions](/docs/build/auth/access/functions) covers each tier, its options and its refusals.

## Workspace-scoped tables

Most of a client app's data belongs to a workspace: the account's personal one, or one a few people share. Stamp the workspace on every row you write, index the table by it, and read through that index, all in the `workspace` tier. [Scope tables to a workspace](/docs/build/data/read-write/workspace-scoped) walks through it, with the check a function needs when it takes a row's id.

## Personal data joins export and erasure

When a person exports their data or deletes their account, the package covers every table it keeps, their billing customer, their email contact and the files they uploaded. A table of yours that holds something of a person (what they wrote, uploaded or set) joins as a source in `backend/account.ts`. A source is an internal query that reads an account's rows for the export, and an internal mutation that erases a batch of them. [Export and erase account data](/docs/build/auth/accounts/data) shows both.

Keep personal data where a source can find it: in a field of the row it belongs to, indexed by the account or the address. Rows that belong to a shared workspace stay when one of its members leaves.

## Your events on the activity trail

Sign-ins, workspace changes, subscriptions and administrator actions land on one activity trail, read per account and per workspace. Add your own events with `audit.recordBackendEvent(ctx, { type: 'app.<kind>', … })` from a mutation or an action. In a mutation, the event commits or rolls back with your own writes. [Record your own events](/docs/build/auth/admin/audit-log#record-your-own-events) has the fields and their limits.

## Why it works this way

A component's tables are sealed, so the package can migrate, prune and erase its own records without a migration of yours, and an upgrade never touches your schema. The cost is that a join across the line goes through a function: you store the package's ids as strings and read its records through its API. Ids arrive from `ctx`, after the tier has checked the caller, so a client can never name an account or a workspace it does not belong to.

## What it is not

- **Not another database.** Everything lives in your Convex deployment ([Convex](/docs/services/convex)). There is no adapter for SQL or another store.
- **Not an ORM.** You write Convex queries and mutations directly; the package adds tiers and helpers around them.
- **Not row-level security.** Each function checks access in code, through its tier and your own checks. The [Roadmap](/docs/start/roadmap) lists what waits for later releases.

## Change it later

- **Add a field** as optional, deploy, fill it in with a [migration](/docs/build/data/jobs/migrations), then make it required.
- **Add an index** to a large table with `staged: true`, so the deploy does not wait for its backfill.
- **Count or sum** rows with an [aggregate](/docs/build/data/read-write/aggregates), never by reading every row.
- **Delete what you no longer need** with a [retention rule](/docs/build/data/retention). The component's maintenance never touches your tables.

[Limits and retention](/docs/reference/backend/limits) gives the windows of the package's own tables. The Convex [limits](https://docs.convex.dev/production/state/limits) apply to every table, yours included.

## What this means for you

- Put your tables in `backend/schema.ts`, and store account and workspace ids as strings.
- Give every exported function a tier, and take ids from `ctx`, never from arguments.
- Add a source in `backend/account.ts` for every table that holds something of a person.
- Pick your deployment's region before you store real data: moving it later means a backup and a restore ([Convex](/docs/services/convex)).
