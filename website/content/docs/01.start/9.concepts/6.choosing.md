`nuxt-backend` suits a Nuxt client app that sells something, whether plans, credits or metered AI, and wants accounts, billing and email working before its first feature. It runs on a realtime backend, with pages you restyle rather than build. It suits an app less when it needs a database other than Convex, passwords or social sign-in, billing per seat, or the service-app features of the [Roadmap](/docs/start/roadmap).

## Compared with the usual ways

| Approach | What you get | What is left to you |
|---|---|---|
| **nuxt-backend** | Sign-in, launch access, workspaces, billing with credits, email, notifications, metered AI, the pages for them, and a realtime database, in one module, with styled pages you can take headless | Your product, and a sandbox account with each provider you use |
| **nuxt-auth-utils with a payments SDK** | Sessions and sign-in providers in Nuxt, passwords included, with payments from the SDK | The database, workspaces, the webhook handling and entitlements, email, and every page |
| **Supabase** | A Postgres database with auth (passwords, social sign-in, links), storage and realtime | Billing, workspaces as a product, email beyond auth, and the pages |
| **A hosted auth service such as Clerk** | Hosted sign-in, user management and organizations, with prebuilt UI | The database, billing entitlements and metering, email, and data that lives with the service |
| **nuxt-convex-module on its own** | The same realtime database and live queries, with the auth plumbing | Everything this package adds: auth flows, workspaces, billing, email, pages, the CLI and doctor |
| **Better Auth, the billing and email providers, wired by hand** | Exactly the parts this package is built from | The glue: webhook verification, the entitlement cache, credit reservation, email templates and delivery tracking, the pages, and keeping it all current |

## The services you commit to

| Service | What it does here | Without an account |
|---|---|---|
| [Convex](/docs/services/convex) | The database, functions, scheduling and file storage | A local deployment, on your machine only |
| [Polar](/docs/services/polar) | Checkout, subscriptions, invoices, credit meters and the customer portal, as merchant of record | Nothing: billing waits for a sandbox token |
| [Resend](/docs/services/resend) | Sending email, its delivery events, topics and inbound mail | Sign-in codes in the terminal of a dev deployment |
| [Convex AI Gateway, or a Vercel AI Gateway key](/docs/services/ai-gateway) (once you sell AI) | Model calls by `provider/model` id, at their real cost | Nothing: a model call is refused until one of them is reachable |
| [Vercel](/docs/services/vercel) (optional) | Hosting first, with BotID's invisible bot check; any host that runs Nuxt works, with a Cloudflare Turnstile challenge instead | The Nuxt dev server, and the bot check's test mode |
| [Sentry](/docs/services/sentry) (optional) | Error reports, traces and cron check-ins | A local error log in DevTools |

Each has a free tier that covers development and an early launch, and their pricing pages say where it ends. The Convex AI Gateway is the exception: it needs a paid Convex plan, and a Vercel AI Gateway key takes its place on the free one. The optional ones change where the app runs and what it reports: without Vercel the app runs on any host Nitro deploys to, and without Sentry the monitoring layer reads `missing` and reports nothing ([Capabilities](/docs/start/concepts/capabilities)). Billing is the one with a cost per sale, since the billing provider takes a fee on each payment it handles as merchant of record. Better Auth, the auth library, runs inside your deployment and costs nothing.

The [Service map](/docs/services) lists each service with its free tier, the env it needs and the order to connect them in.

## Where your data lives

- **In your deployment:** accounts, sessions, passkeys, workspaces, members and invitations; your own tables and files; the cache of each customer's plans, benefits and balances; email delivery records, the activity trail and feature flags. `npx convex export` takes a snapshot of all of it.
- **At the billing provider:** customers, subscriptions, orders, invoices and credit usage, the records of truth for money, readable through its API and dashboard.
- **At the email provider:** the messages sent and their delivery events, for as long as it keeps them.

## Replacing a provider

The public API never names a provider: your code calls `useCredits`, `billing.requireFeature` and `email.send`, and reads `BILLING_*` and `EMAIL_*`. A different provider is therefore a change inside the package, not in your app, but it is not a setting: each integration is written against its provider's own capabilities, and the package ships one per layer. What does move with a provider is its data: subscriptions and customers stay with the billing provider you leave.
