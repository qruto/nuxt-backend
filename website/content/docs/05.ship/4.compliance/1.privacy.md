What an app built on the package keeps of a person, where, for how long, and what export and erasure do with it. Use it to write your privacy notice and to answer a compliance review. Every table below is generated from the code: the data inventory, the subprocessors, the data map and the cookies. The package also serves your [legal pages](#legal-pages) from the same facts, asks before any optional storage with a consent manager, and erases a person at every provider in one cascade.

::warning
**Not legal advice.** The GDPR makes you the controller of your users' data. The package gives you the facts about its own part. Your privacy notice, your legal bases and your agreements with each provider stay yours: have them reviewed.
::

Everything the package keeps of a person is a participant of one registry, `ACCOUNT_DATA_SOURCES` in `nuxt-backend/account`. An account's [export](/docs/build/auth/accounts/data#export-an-accounts-data) reads it, its [erasure](/docs/build/auth/accounts/data#delete-an-account) runs it, and the tables below are generated from it. So a participant added in a release appears here without an edit. Your own tables join through `setupAccount({ sources })`, and belong in your privacy notice beside these.

The consent ledger is the `consents` participant, and the deletion grace period the `deletions` one ([Consents and deletion](#consents-and-deletion)).

## Legal pages

The package serves four public pages, filled from your company's details, your brand's name and the capabilities your app uses. Each page asks the app's health route (`/api/backend/health`) which capabilities are in use, with the server render: a capability whose state is `missing` is left out, and when the backend can't be reached every capability counts, so a page lists too much rather than leave a processor out. Each is a packaged page like `/login`: move it, turn it off, or replace it with your own.

| Page key | Default path | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| `privacy` | `/legal/privacy` | Who you are and how to reach you, the data map with each retention window, the subprocessors, the cookies and storage, and how a person exports, corrects or deletes their data |
| `terms` | `/legal/terms` | Your company and contact, accounts and sign-in, plans and renewal and the merchant of record's role while billing is in use, the content rules with a link to the notice form, and AI features while they are in use |
| `subprocessors` | `/legal/subprocessors` | The generated list above, for the capabilities your app turns on |
| `accessibility` | `/legal/accessibility` | Your accessibility statement: the standard, how it is checked, the known gaps, and where to report a barrier ([Accessibility](/docs/ship/compliance/accessibility)) |

Give the pages your details in app config:

```ts [app/app.config.ts]
export default defineAppConfig({
  backend: {
    legal: {
      company: 'Panels Studio Ltd',
      address: '1 Example Street, Dublin D02 XY45, Ireland',
      contact: 'privacy@panels.example',
      dpo: 'dpo@panels.example',
    },
  },
})
```

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `company` | Your legal entity's name, as the controller (GDPR Art. 13(1)(a)) |
| `address` | Its postal address |
| `contact` | Where people send privacy requests and report an accessibility barrier: an email address or a URL |
| `dpo` | Your data protection officer's contact, when you must appoint one (GDPR Art. 37); leave it out otherwise |
| `host` | The host your app runs on, when it is not Vercel: `{ name, location, agreement }`. The pages list it in place of Vercel's row, with your `location` as written |

Move or turn off a page in `nuxt.config`:

```ts [nuxt.config.ts]
export default defineNuxtConfig({
  modules: ['nuxt-backend'],
  backend: {
    pages: {
      terms: '/terms', // serve it at another path
      accessibility: false, // you publish your own statement
    },
  },
})
```

Each page links the others, and leaves out a detail you didn't set; in development it names the `legal` keys still missing. Every word is a label of the `legal` group, the data map's, processors' and stored items' rows included, so you change or translate any of them like other copy ([Change copy and labels](/docs/build/ui/customize/labels)).

All four are marked "not legal advice" by the `legal.disclaimer` label above each page; once your lawyer reviewed the pages, set it to an empty string. They are a starting point drawn from what the package knows, not a policy a lawyer wrote. What they can't know stays yours to write: the purposes and legal bases of your own features, your own tables, your governing law and liability terms, and your refund policy. Point your required [consents](/docs/build/auth/accounts/consents) at these paths, and raise a consent's version when you change a page.

## Cookies and browser storage

Storage that is strictly necessary for the service a person asked for needs no consent (ePrivacy Directive Art. 5(3)). Everything else does, and the EDPB reads "storage or access" broadly: [Guidelines 2/2023](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-22023-on-technical-scope-of-art-53-of-eprivacy-directive_en) cover local storage, pixels and fingerprinting, not only cookies.

## Consents and deletion

Both are participants of the registry:

- **Consents.** Each consent a person gives, to your terms, your privacy policy or a marketing topic, is a row of a versioned ledger: what they agreed to, which version, and when they gave or withdrew it. A consent given before sign-up, such as on a waitlist, is kept by a hash of the address and carried over to the account. An export includes the ledger, and an erasure covers it like every other participant ([Consents](/docs/build/auth/accounts/consents)).
- **Deletion with a grace period.** Asking to delete an account schedules its erasure instead of running it at once. The person gets an email with a link to keep the account, and can cancel until the grace period ends. Then the erasure runs every participant above ([Export and delete account data](/docs/build/auth/accounts/data)).

## The erasure cascade

One erasure reaches every place the package put a person's data. It runs in the order below, and resumes where it stopped if a step fails ([Requests about personal data](#requests-about-personal-data)).

| Where | What the erasure does | When |
|---|---|---|
| Your Convex deployment | Deletes or pseudonymizes every participant's rows, as the inventory says | Today |
| The files they uploaded | Deletes them from file storage | Today |
| The email provider's contact | Deletes the contact, with its segments and topics | Today |
| The billing provider's customer | Deletes it with `anonymize=true`, which keeps its orders for the provider's books ([Polar](https://polar.sh/docs/api-reference/2027-01/customers/delete-customer-by-external-id)) | Today |
| The monitoring service | Reports name no account once it is deleted. What was reported keeps its scrubbed contents until the service's retention removes it: its per-user deletion is not confirmed | Today |
| The email component's records | Expire with their retention, 7 to 30 days | Today |
| The mounted billing component's sync | Kept: it has no delete yet, and names ids only | Upstream gap |

## Requests about personal data

Answer a request within one month (GDPR Art. 12(3)).

- **Access.** A signed-in person exports everything from `/security`: one file, with the files they uploaded, behind a link that works for an hour, at most three a day. An administrator acting as the account can't.
- **Rectification.** A person changes their name, address and language on `/profile` ([Profile](/docs/build/auth/accounts/profile)).
- **Erasure.** Deleting the account from `/security` runs every participant above once its grace period ends. An administrator removes an account from the admin console the same way. For someone without an account (a gift's recipient, an invitee, a contact, someone who sent a notice on the report form), run `account.forgetEmail(ctx, email)` from an internal mutation of yours. It matches the address exactly, trimmed and lower-cased: a notice sent from `me+shop@example.com` is erased by that address, not by `me@example.com`, so erase each address the person names.
- **A failed erasure.** A participant that keeps failing stops the erasure there and logs `account.erasure_failed`. Running `account.forget` for the account again resumes it ([Respond to incidents](/docs/ship/operate/runbooks#an-erasure-stopped)).
- **Retention.** Every window the package enforces is in [Limits and retention](/docs/reference/backend/limits#windows-and-lifetimes).

## Data residency and transfers

Where each provider keeps data, and what you choose, checked on 2026-10-25:

| Service | Where data lives | Your choice |
|---|---|---|
| Convex | The deployment's region: US by default, EU (Dublin), Canada or Australia | Chosen when you create the deployment. The EU region costs more, and moving a deployment means a backup and a restore ([Convex](/docs/services/convex)). |
| Resend | Mail is sent from the region you pick; account data, contacts and logs stay in the US | `EMAIL_REGION`: `us-east-1`, `eu-west-1`, `sa-east-1` or `ap-northeast-1` (0.2.0) ([Resend](/docs/services/resend)) |
| Polar | A US company, Polar Software, Inc. | None |
| Vercel | Functions run in the region you set; a US company | The project's function region. The Vercel AI Gateway can pin inference to the EU ([AI gateway](/docs/services/ai-gateway)). |
| Sentry | The US or the EU data region | Chosen when you create the organization ([Sentry](/docs/services/sentry)) |

Personal data that leaves the EU needs a transfer tool (GDPR Chapter V). For US providers certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, the Commission's [adequacy decision](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2023/1795/oj) covers it. The General Court upheld that decision in 2025, and an appeal, C-703/25 P, is pending at the Court of Justice ([summary](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20251201-european-court-of-justice-to-review-challenge-to-eu-us-data-privacy-framework)). Keep the [standard contractual clauses](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2021/914/oj) in each agreement as a fallback, and check whether each provider is certified.

## What stays yours

- **Your privacy notice** (GDPR Art. 13): the packaged page is a start, and your own features and tables belong in it.
- **A legal basis for each purpose** (Art. 6): contract for the account and billing, consent for marketing and replay, and your own analysis for the rest.
- **Your record of processing activities** (Art. 30), built from the data map.
- **A data protection impact assessment** where processing is likely high risk (Art. 35). Generating images from people's photos may be.
- **Breach notification** to your authority within 72 hours (Art. 33) ([Respond to incidents](/docs/ship/operate/runbooks)).
- **An EU representative** when your company is outside the EU and serves people in it (Art. 27).
- **California:** a notice at collection and the right to opt out of sale or sharing, once the CCPA's thresholds reach you ([CCPA](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa)).
