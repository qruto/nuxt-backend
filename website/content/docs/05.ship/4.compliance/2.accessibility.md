The European Accessibility Act has applied since 2025-06-28 to services sold online to consumers in the EU. In practice it asks for WCAG 2.1 level AA, plus a public statement of how your service meets the requirements. The package's pages and components are built and tested against WCAG levels A and AA. Your own pages, your colors and your statement are yours.

::warning
**Not legal advice.** This summarizes [Directive (EU) 2019/882](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj) as it read on 2026-10-25. Each country enforces it through its own law and authority, so check the rules where you sell.
::

## What the act asks

- **Who it covers.** E-commerce services: services sold at a distance, through a website or an app, to conclude a contract with a consumer (Art. 2(2)(f) and Art. 3(30)). A client app that sells plans or credits to the public is likely one. An app sold only to businesses is likely not.
- **Who is exempt.** A microenterprise that provides services: fewer than 10 people, and an annual turnover or balance sheet of at most €2 million (Art. 3(23) and Art. 4(5)). Ireland's regulator explains the test in its [guidelines for microenterprises](https://www.ccpc.ie/business/enforcement/accessibility/european-accessibility-act-guidelines-for-microenterprises/).
- **What "accessible" means.** The requirements are in Annex I. Meeting the harmonised standard, EN 301 549, presumes conformity (Art. 15). Its web chapter is WCAG 2.1 levels A and AA.
- **What you publish.** Information on how the service meets the requirements, in your terms or an equivalent document (Art. 13(2) and Annex V). Most apps publish it as an accessibility statement.
- **When it would cost too much.** You may claim a disproportionate burden for a requirement, but you must assess and document it, and tell the authority when it asks (Art. 14).

## Who answers for what

| The package | You |
|---|---|
| Packaged pages and components that work with a keyboard, a screen reader and speech input ([Accessibility](/docs/build/ui/accessibility)) | Every page and component of your own |
| Default tokens that pass WCAG AA contrast in light and dark | The contrast of any token you change |
| `<html lang>` on packaged pages | The language of your own pages |
| An axe-core check of the starter's pages, in both color schemes, on every change | The same check on your pages |
| A packaged accessibility statement page, at `/legal/accessibility` | What the statement says, and a way for people to report a barrier |

## Check your pages

::steps{level="3"}

### Run an automated check on every page

Add axe-core to your end-to-end tests ([Run end-to-end tests](/docs/develop/testing/e2e)):

```bash
npm install -D @playwright/test @axe-core/playwright
```

```ts [tests/e2e/accessibility.spec.ts]
import AxeBuilder from '@axe-core/playwright'
import { expect, test } from '@playwright/test'

const paths = ['/', '/login', '/pricing', '/strips']

for (const path of paths) {
  test(`${path} has no WCAG A or AA violations`, async ({ page }) => {
    await page.goto(path)
    const results = await new AxeBuilder({ page })
      .withTags(['wcag2a', 'wcag2aa', 'wcag21a', 'wcag21aa', 'wcag22aa'])
      .analyze()
    expect(results.violations).toEqual([])
  })
}
```

Run it in both color schemes: set `colorScheme: 'dark'` in a second Playwright project. You should see every test pass. A failure names the rule, the element and how to fix it.

### Walk each flow with the keyboard

Sign in, buy a plan, create something and delete it, with Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, Space and Escape only. Focus must stay visible, follow the reading order, and never get trapped.

### Listen with a screen reader

Run the same flows with VoiceOver on macOS or iOS, or NVDA on Windows. Every control should say what it does, and every change should be announced. Automated checks miss most of this.

### Check your colors and media

Check the contrast of every token you changed ([Restyle with tokens](/docs/build/ui/customize/tokens)): 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text and controls. Give each image an `alt` that says what it shows, or `alt=""` when it is decoration. Caption your videos.

### Check your emails

Emails are part of the service. Give images alt text, use real headings, and keep the plain-text part ([Email templates](/docs/build/email/templates)).

::

## Publish an accessibility statement

Your statement tells people how the service meets the requirements and what to do when it doesn't. Annex V asks for a description of the service and how it works, and how it meets the accessibility requirements. A useful statement also gives:

- the standard you follow (EN 301 549, which is WCAG 2.1 AA) and how you checked it;
- the parts that don't meet it yet, and when they will;
- how to report a barrier, and how fast you answer;
- the authority people can complain to in your country;
- the date you last reviewed it.

The package serves a statement at the `accessibility` page key, `/legal/accessibility`, filled from your company's details ([Legal pages](/docs/ship/compliance/privacy#legal-pages)). It names WCAG 2.1 AA as the standard, says how the packaged pages are tested, and asks people to report a barrier to `appConfig.backend.legal.contact`. Its words are the `legal.accessibility` labels: change the known gaps and the answer time to yours, and link the page from your footer. To write the whole statement yourself, put a page of your own at its path or turn the key off.

## Limits

- **No tool proves conformance.** Automated checks find some problems, not all. Only testing with people, ideally people who use assistive technology, gets close.
- **WCAG 2.2 is ahead of the standard.** The package tests WCAG 2.2 AA rules, which include 2.1 AA. EN 301 549 still points at 2.1.
- **The statement is a claim.** Whatever it says, an authority can check it against your app.
